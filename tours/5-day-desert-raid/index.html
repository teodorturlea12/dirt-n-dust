06Good to know

Questions riders ask.

Packing, distances, cash and transfers — answered by the crew that runs the tour.

What do I need to bring?+

We provide the bikes, fuel, accommodation and logistics — you just need to bring your riding gear, personal items and sense of adventure! Here's a checklist to help you prepare:

Essential documents

  • Full motorcycle licence (valid at the destination)
  • Passport, valid for at least 6 months
  • Travel and medical insurance, including motorcycling cover

Recommended riding gear

  • Helmet (mandatory)
  • Enduro / off-road boots
  • Gloves
  • Body armour and knee protection
  • Goggles or glasses — we recommend goggles because of the dust
  • Hydration pack or CamelBak

If you don't have your own riding gear, we can arrange rentals — but we must know well in advance! We'll always make sure we've got something in your size, but if too many people need the same size and we need to grow our gear selection, we need time.

Personal kit

  • Lightweight clothing for the evenings
  • Fleece or jacket — it can get a little cold, even in the desert
  • Sunglasses, sunscreen, lip balm
  • Toiletries and any medication you need
  • Small day bag or backpack
  • Device chargers — Morocco and the Balkans use European two-pin sockets, so bring a plug adapter if you need one

We provide a 30-litre duffle bag for your personal items and extra gear during the tour, while your main travel bag stays back at our base — space in the support 4×4 is limited. Waterproofs are rarely needed; we monitor the weather before your tour and let you know if they'd be a worthwhile inclusion.

How far do we ride each day?+

Daily distances vary depending on the route and terrain, but most days you can expect to cover between 120 and 250 kilometres. On open desert pistes we might cover more ground, while technical mountain trails or sandy sections can make for shorter but more intense riding days.

We typically ride 5–7 hours per day, including plenty of breaks for photos, refreshments and lunch. The pace is always adjusted to the group's ability and comfort — the goal is to enjoy the ride, not race through it.

How do I get there?+

Direct flights to Ouarzazate run from Paris, Marseille and Barcelona. Marrakech has direct connections from Gatwick, Luton, Stansted, Manchester, Liverpool, Edinburgh, Bristol and Newcastle.

What happens if I damage the bike?+

Scratches and scuffs are part of riding and tolerated. Broken parts are charged at parts price. A damage deposit of €600 / £500 applies.

What if I have dietary requirements?+

Please let us know well in advance and we will do our best to accommodate you. Trying to deal with these things at short notice is never easy, so please don't expect everything to work out if you don't give us time to organise!

What age do I need to be?+

Anyone above 18 years of age can join a tour solo. Below that, you require parental permission and an accompanying adult.

Can I rent riding gear?+

Yes — helmet, boots and full body armour can be rented per day (full set €30/day, single items €10/day). Let us know well in advance so we can have your sizes ready.

Morocco specifics

How safe is Morocco?+

Morocco is one of the safest and most welcoming countries in North Africa. The Moroccan people are known for their hospitality, and tourism is a major part of the economy, so visitors are well looked after.

The areas we ride in are peaceful and far from any trouble spots. We keep up to date with local conditions and plan all our routes accordingly.

As with any trip abroad, normal travel awareness applies: keep your valuables secure, respect local customs, and follow your guide's advice. On our tours, your safety is our top priority — from well-maintained bikes and backup vehicles to experienced guides who know the terrain and local culture inside out.

What about cash?+

Morocco uses the Dirham (dh / MAD) — roughly 12 dh to the pound and 10 dh to the euro. It is a largely cash society and you can never rely on being able to pay by card.

As most costs on tour are covered by us, there is little need to sort your own cash. We're happy to cover your costs even outside of what's included and settle up at the end to make life easy. If you do want to carry your own cash:

  • The Dirham is a closed currency — getting it outside the country before you travel is extremely difficult
  • Airport currency exchanges often charge a lot and offer poor rates
  • The easiest and often cheapest way is an ATM — most medium-size towns have one
  • Dirham left over at the end? We're usually able to exchange it with your home currency
How about transfer to and from the airport?+

Although usually not included, we'll be in touch before your trip to help organise transfers for either end of your trip in Morocco. We're usually able to combine your transfer with other riders, so costs rarely exceed €40 each way between Marrakech and Ouarzazate. If you're flying into Ouarzazate directly, we'll pick you up ourselves.
